Liquidation Preference: Determines the payout order and amount investors receive before founders if the company is sold, goes bankrupt, or liquidates.

Anti-dilution: Protects investors from losing value if the company later issues new shares at a lower price (a "down round").

Board (of Directors): Defines the composition of the company's board and specifies how many seats are controlled by investors versus founders.

Protective Provisions: Gives investors veto rights over specific major corporate actions, such as selling the company, taking on debt, or changing the core business.

Vesting: The schedule dictating how founders and employees "earn" their shares over time (typically a 4-year period with a 1-year cliff) to ensure they stay with the company.

Information Rights: Guarantees investors the right to receive regular financial statements, budgets, and operational updates from the startup.

ROFR / Co-sale (Right of First Refusal): If a founder attempts to sell their shares, this gives investors the right to either buy those shares first (ROFR) or participate in the sale alongside the founder (Co-sale).

Drag-along: A mechanism where if a majority of shareholders agree to sell the company, they can force the minority shareholders to agree to the sale under the same terms.

Option Pool: The specific percentage of the company's equity set aside to grant stock options to future employees and advisors.

Exclusivity / No-shop: A binding agreement preventing the founders from negotiating with or soliciting offers from other investors for a set period (usually 30–45 days) while the current deal is finalized.

Conversion: The right of investors to convert their preferred shares into standard common shares, usually exercised if an IPO or highly profitable sale occurs.

Redemption: A clause giving investors the right to demand that the company buy back their shares at the original purchase price after a certain number of years.

Other: Any boilerplate legal text, definitions, or miscellaneous provisions that do not fit into the core economic or control categories above.
